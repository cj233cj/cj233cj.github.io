Takaichi is the conservative Japanese prime minister who believes a woman should adopt her husband's family name after marriage.

It's no coincidence that her party (LDP, 自民党) allies with Ishin no Kai (JIP, 維新). "Ishin" is a historical *name* that bears the meaning of learning every aspect of lifestyle from the Western world, from drinking coffee to wives taking husbands' surnames.

It's also pretty funny, because women didn't even have family names in ancient Japan despite their positions in the social hierarchy, which makes "having a family name" a truly huge progressive drift away from convention.

Conservatism is a relative thing especially when the progressive path is long enough to become a tradition of its own.

Nowadays, as a common sense around gender equality, women are supposed to keep their original family names whether married or not. However, family names are indisputably still patriarchal identifications aka fathers' names in every country. Which means keeping one's original family name practically contributes nothing to the feminist narrative about individual independence or non-patriarchal family structures.

Family names simply are not a progressive thing overall.

Ideally, the freedom to change one's name is or should be a an expression of free will or "individual autonomy", something essentially ingrained in basic human rights. But it's actually not popular at all in modern Japan or most democratic countries mostly because people have bank accounts with their and their fathers names attached.
